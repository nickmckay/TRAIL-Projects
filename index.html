TRAIL Projects

Teaching, Research, and AI Learning — Northern Arizona University

TRAIL (Transformation through Artificial Intelligence in Learning) is a Northern Arizona University program, sponsored by the Office of the Provost, that funds faculty to explore generative AI in their teaching and research. The program runs two tracks: an Instructional Track focused on student learning outcomes and a Research & Scholarship Track focused on advancing faculty research using AI methods.

The projects below represent two cohort years. The 2024-25 cohort was exclusively instructional, with 10 faculty projects spanning disciplines from Spanish language learning to software engineering. The 2025-26 cohort expanded the program to include a dedicated Research & Scholarship Track alongside 14 new instructional projects across six NAU colleges.

The 2026-27 cohort is now underway, with 10 instructional track awards and 4 research track awards. The instructional projects alone will directly reach roughly 1,300 students in about 20 courses across seven NAU colleges; see the 2026-27 cohort section below for each project and the courses and programs it affects. Project pages will be added as the work unfolds.

2026-27 Cohort

Fourteen new projects launched in fall 2026: 10 instructional and 4 research awards. Student impact was an explicit selection criterion, and every instructional proposal identified the courses and enrollments it would affect. Together, the instructional projects will directly reach roughly 1,300 students in about 20 courses across seven NAU colleges, from first-year writing and Calculus to MBA analytics, teacher preparation, and health sciences. Presentations and project pages will appear here after the spring 2027 showcase.

Instructional Track

Developing an AI Mathematics Grading Agent for Enhanced Student Feedback and Instructional Efficiency

An AI agent that grades handwritten math homework and quizzes, giving students in large-enrollment courses fast, personalized feedback.

Piloting fall 2026 in courses from Calculus (MAT 136/137/238) through Linear Algebra and Combinatorics; roughly 100 students.

Misha Baltushkin, Dana Ernst, Monika Keindl, and Nandor Sieben, Mathematics & Statistics

More Human Than Ever: Filmmaking with AI

AI joins the classroom as a simulated participant whose scripts and shot lists become a foil for students to define their own creative voice.

CMF 129 (Introduction to Screenwriting) and CMF 235 (Filmmaking Techniques), two sections each; about 80 students.

Xin Yi Bao, Creative Media and Film

AI-Powered Interview Training for Requirements Elicitation in Software Engineering

LLM-simulated stakeholders give students repeated, realistic practice conducting requirements interviews, with structured personalized feedback.

CS 386 (Software Engineering), required for CS and SE majors; more than 100 students per year.

Marco A. Gerosa, SICCS

Advancing Ethical AI Literacy Through Writing Center Innovation

AI-informed tutoring, workshops, and course integrations across the Lumberjack Writing Center, including a specially trained cohort of AI-specialist writing assistants.

ENG 100, ENG 405, and ENG 436/536 (about 400 students), plus 3,500 to 4,000 Writing Center appointments per year.

Alana Kuhlman, Lumberjack Writing Center

The Pillars of Intelligence: A Foundational Mathematical Sequence for the AI Era

Two new courses, The Linear Language of AI and The Logic of Learning, teach the linear algebra, probability, and logic underneath modern AI systems.

New permanent courses in NAU's AI major and certificate; 60 students in year one, with enrollment growth expected.

Benjamin Lucas, Dana Ernst, and Ian Williams, Mathematics & Statistics

Redesigning an Educational Technology Course to Expand Future Teachers' Critically Conscious, Career-Ready GAI Literacies

A phased redesign of the required educational technology course so future PreK-12 teachers build both practical AI skills and critical literacies.

ETC 447 (Technology for Teachers), required for all elementary and early childhood education majors; scaling to about 300 pre-service teachers per year across 12 sections.

Catharyn Shelton, Educational Specialties

Reducing Student Resistance to AI Through Structured Literacy, Transparency, and Ethical Integration

A two-phase intervention targeting low AI confidence, distrust, and ethical concerns through structured literacy training and transparent use guidelines.

PSY 290 (Research Methods), two sections in spring 2027; about 150 students.

BAN 442/542 (Descriptive Analytics and Visualization), about 50 students, extending to the MBA course BA 642.
